# Future-State Journey: Alexandra

*Based on fictional sample data (Riverty First Consumer Card practice pack); not Riverty research.*

**Persona:** Alexandra, the heavy BNPL user who wants Riverty in the shop.
**Goal:** Use pay later at the supermarket and other shops, without a new thing to check before payday (N-04, N-02).
**Strategy:** Riverty extends from a checkout-only pay later option into a card the consumer carries, so heavy BNPL users can pay later anywhere they shop, and Riverty keeps the transaction and the relationship that would otherwise go to her bank card.

## Timeline at a glance

```
[1] Card ready  --->  [2] Tap and pay  --->  [3] Know it went through  --->  [4] See what is owed,
    before the till       in the shop                                              decide later
```

## Stages

### 1. Card ready before the till
- **User Action:** Adds card to wallet from the approval screen -> taps to pay the same day.
- **Internal State:** Relieved, with nothing extra to remember.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** 143 of 431 activated cards never reached the wallet (T-102), so the card could not compete with the bank card she taps by reflex (N-03).

### 2. Tap and pay in the shop
- **User Action:** Taps phone at the supermarket -> pays like she would with her bank card.
- **User Action:** Decline shows reason and retry hint -> she fixes it at the till.
- **Internal State:** Unthinking and confident, not exposed in the queue.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Unexplained in-store decline, 48 logged and 8.7% in Month 4 (T-101, N-06).

### 3. Know it went through
- **User Action:** Receives instant purchase confirmation -> no reason to pay again with another card.
- **Internal State:** Reassured about the charge.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Late push notifications led participants to pay twice (T-108).

### 4. See what is owed, decide later
- **User Action:** Opens one balance and due date -> knows her total before payday.
- **User Action:** Chooses pay now or later after buying -> money leaves once she keeps the item.
- **Internal State:** In control, with no extra app to check.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Two screens, two reminders for different amounts (T-103, T-104, N-07), the "one more thing to check" burden (N-02).

## Three competitive advantages over the debit-card workaround

1. **Keeps the deferral:** Pay later at the till -> she gets the benefit the debit card removes (N-01, N-04).
2. **Fails with an explanation:** A decline states its reason -> the card is retried, not abandoned (N-06).
3. **Keeps the relationship:** Transaction stays on Riverty -> the data and habit the debit card hid from Riverty stay visible.

## Limits to know

- **Value proposition was cut off.** It ended after "...they've decided to keep the item" and named Lena while the persona is Alexandra. Lena and Alexandra were treated as the same persona.
- **Stage 4's pay-now-or-later choice is a design idea, not an observation.** It matches backlog item B4; no participant tried it.
- **Stage 2 assumes the card works beyond partner merchants.** Partner merchants carry 63.1% of volume, and no note covers non-partner failures.
- **Advantage 3 is part hypothesis.** The research shows reversion to the debit card, not to Klarna or PayPal.
- **N-06 has no stated age or BNPL profile.** Alexandra is a composite of N-01, N-02, N-04 and N-06.
